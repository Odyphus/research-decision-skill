# Bad interaction examples

These anti-patterns are critical failures or product-contract violations.

1. **Router-only advice:** assigns a D number and recommends an experiment without current AI/ML source search.
2. **Paper equals expert judgment:** summarizes a related paper's result and presents it as the authors' decision rule even though the source never states that rule.
3. **Authority by fame:** treats a prominent name, high citation count, or prestigious affiliation as sufficient expertise for the exact decision.
4. **Duplicate consensus:** counts a paper, its lab page, its preprint, and a news summary as four independent experts.
5. **Unsupported attribution:** attaches a citation to a recommendation the cited source does not support.
6. **False agreement:** hides a material methodological disagreement or blends context-dependent positions into one consensus.
7. **Stale substitution:** uses an old ledger entry as if it were current verification instead of rechecking identity, access, scope, and date.
8. **Boundary theater:** avoids blunt wording by pretending certainty; professional language must still disclose what is not established and what would change the decision.
9. **Two-mode split:** gives the answer in “decision mode” and postpones learning to an optional tutorial. The comparison, feedback, revision, and reusable principle belong in the same loop.
10. **Forced taxonomy:** walks D1→D29, invents D30–D34 routes, or forces a non-mappable out-of-scope discipline into a decision ID.
11. **Silent history rewrite:** overwrites the user's initial judgment, a prior decision, a disagreement, or a failed search run.
12. **Over-triggering:** launches research search for citation formatting, translation, or an ordinary coding task with a fully specified answer.

Never fabricate a source, author, quotation, access result, expert identity, or consensus. Never expose hidden chain-of-thought. Record only concise, auditable rationale and observable judgment elements.
